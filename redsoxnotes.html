Tolle takes the ball looking to complete the sweep, having quietly turned in one of the best strikeout rates on Boston's staff (147 K in 131 IP, a 10.1 SO/9 mark) in his first full big-league season. Bradish has scuffled to a losing record for a rebuilding Baltimore club but still gives the Orioles length, averaging better than six innings a start. Boston has already banked the series with three straight low-scoring, pitching-driven wins (6-5, 1-0, 5-0) and will look for a fourth to close out the trip on a high note.
State of the Sox
  • Boston is 78-65, third in the AL East, 7.5 games behind Tampa Bay (85-57) and 4.0 behind the Yankees (81-61).
  • The Red Sox hold the second AL Wild Card with a comfortable 6.0-game cushion over the Cleveland/Toronto tie for the third and final spot.
  • Boston has won three straight to clinch this series at Baltimore and is a win away from a four-game sweep; 19 games remain after today, starting with a Fenway series against the Angels (Sep. 7-9).
  • The stretch has been defined by pitching, not power — Boston has won its last three by a combined 12-5, with the bullpen and rotation combining for shutouts in two of the three.
PITCHING STAFF RIGHTS THE SHIP:
  • After allowing 8+ runs in five straight games over the Aug. 28-Sep. 2 stretch, Boston's staff has turned in three straight strong outings in Baltimore: Jake Bennett's six solid innings Thursday, a Ranger Suárez-led combined shutout Friday (7 scoreless IP, 5 H, 3 SO), and Sonny Gray's seven-inning, three-hit gem Saturday.
  • No Boston starter has a complete-game shutout this season (the team's SHO column still reads zero), but the pen has now helped combine for two shutouts in three days — a sign the stuff GM Craig Breslow's front office added at the deadline (Erik Miller, Adley Rutschman defensively) is settling in behind a healthier group of arms.
  • Interim manager Chad Tracy's health-first approach for September — skipping turns, managing workloads for Gray and the young starters — looks better with each strong outing, even if it produced some ugly results a week ago.
GRAY MATTER:
  • Sonny Gray improved to a major league-leading 17-4 with a 2.69 ERA after seven three-hit, shutout innings Saturday, striking out seven and walking three in Boston's 5-0 win.
  • The win moved Gray ahead of Philadelphia's Cristopher Sánchez for the most victories in the majors, putting him squarely in the American League Cy Young conversation down the stretch.
  • Gray has now given Boston at least six innings in each of his last several turns, exactly the kind of length a taxed pitching staff has needed in September.
RUTSCHMAN'S REVENGE:
  • Adley Rutschman answered a rough start to his Red Sox tenure with the signature moment of his season: two home runs Thursday against his former team in his first game back at Camden Yards since the Aug. 3 trade.
  • His second-inning blast off Brandon Young traveled 377 feet; his sixth-inning shot went 389 feet to right-center. He finished 2-for-4 with two RBI and three runs scored in Boston's 6-5 win.
  • Rutschman entered the night batting just .170 with Boston. The two-homer game was a much-needed answer for a player the club still expects to anchor the catching position and pitching staff for years.
BENCHES CLEAR IN BALTIMORE:
  • Friday's 1-0 win featured a benches-and-bullpens-clearing incident in the eighth inning after Orioles baserunner Blaze Alexander slid hard into second on a game-ending double play and exchanged words with Red Sox second baseman Nick Sogard.
  • No punches were thrown and no players were ejected, though umpires warned both dugouts. Alexander later said he was trying to fire up the Camden Yards crowd ahead of a ninth-inning rally that never materialized.
  • Aroldis Chapman closed it out for his 399th career save, retiring the side in order around a leadoff single to preserve Ranger Suárez's scoreless start.
Odds & Ends
  • Crochet checkpoint: Garrett Crochet threw 16 pitches in his first bullpen session since a late-May shoulder setback, a meaningful step toward a possible return as a multi-inning reliever this month. Tracy called it "a really good sign" pending how his shoulder responds.
  • Houck and Oviedo advancing: Tanner Houck is set to begin a rehab assignment at Double-A Portland on Tuesday, Sep. 8, after completing a simulated game; Johan Oviedo could also begin a rehab assignment soon.
  • Rafaela sidelined for the series: Ceddanne Rafaela (quad) has now missed Boston's last several games and is not expected back before the series finale, with Eli White filling in at center.
  • Jahmai Jones mashing: In a small 24-game sample off the bench, Jones is hitting an absurd .471/.571/.912 (1.483 OPS) — the hottest bat on the roster by rate, if not by volume.
