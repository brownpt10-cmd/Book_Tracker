PITCHING STAFF UNRAVELS AT THE WORST TIME:
  • Boston's pitching staff has allowed 8 or more runs in five consecutive games — the first time that's happened to a Red Sox staff since 2020 — a stretch that has produced six losses in the last eight games heading into Baltimore.
  • Patrick Sandoval was the latest to get roughed up, allowing 3 earned runs over 4 innings in Wednesday's 8-3 loss to Seattle before Brayan Bello surrendered four more in relief; Sandoval fell to 1-4, 4.41 ERA on the year.
  • Interim manager Chad Tracy has largely resisted panic, noting the club is prioritizing health for Sonny Gray and its young starters even at the cost of some ugly results in September — but the staff will need to right itself against a Baltimore lineup that's 1.5 games from a Wild Card spot of its own.
ANTHONY'S RED-HOT RETURN:
  • Roman Anthony has been Boston's brightest spot since returning from a nearly four-month finger injury, going 9-for-18 (.500) with four extra-base hits over his first four games back.
  • He capped the stretch with his first Fenway home run of the season in Wednesday's loss to Seattle, giving the lineup a jolt even as the pitching staff struggled around him.
  • Anthony is back in an everyday right field/DH role and batting near the top of the order as Boston looks for offensive difference-makers down the stretch.
BACK TO CAMDEN YARDS:
  • Adley Rutschman faces his former club for the first time since Boston acquired him from Baltimore in the Aug. 3 trade — a deal that sent catcher Carlos Narváez and pitching prospects back to the Orioles.
  • Rutschman missed Boston's last three games with a hyperextended right elbow; he said this week he's "trending in the right direction" and is hopeful to catch tonight in Baltimore.
  • He's batting .170 with a .500 OPS across his first 15 games in a Red Sox uniform, a rough start to his Boston tenure the club still hopes turns around behind his defensive reputation and pitching-staff rapport.
Odds & Ends
