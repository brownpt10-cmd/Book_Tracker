State of the Sox
  • Boston is 80-67, third in the AL East, 8.0 games behind Tampa Bay (87-58) and 4.0 behind the Yankees (83-62).
  • The Red Sox hold the second AL Wild Card with a 6.0-game cushion over Cleveland, which holds the third and final Wild Card spot outright. Baseball-Reference's live playoff-odds model (as of Sept. 9) puts Boston at >99.9% to make the postseason and 9.2% to win the World Series.
  • Boston dropped two of three to the last-place Angels to close the homestand, extending a two-game losing streak — the Red Sox's first back-to-back losses since an Aug. 8-12 skid. Thursday is a scheduled off day before a 15-game sprint to the finish.
  • Fifteen games remain, including a decisive road trip to Tampa Bay (Sept. 18-20) and a season-ending homestand against Cleveland and the Cubs.
Angels Series Slips Away
  • Zach Neto, Jose Siri and Denzer Guzman all homered as Los Angeles broke open a scoreless game with a five-run third inning Wednesday, beating Boston 6-4 to take two of three at Fenway.
  • Jake Bennett fell to 9-7 after allowing all five third-inning runs; Roman Anthony and Wilyer Abreu both went deep for Boston in the loss.
  • The Angels (56-90, eliminated from postseason contention) won back-to-back games for the first time since mid-August, a rare bright spot in a lost season for Los Angeles.
Crochet's Bullpen Push
  • Garrett Crochet threw his second side session in five days Tuesday — roughly 20 pitches of fastballs and complementary offerings — and interim manager Chad Tracy said he "looked good and felt good."
  • With Triple-A Worcester's season ending Sept. 20, a full Minor League rehab assignment looks unlikely; Boston may instead have Crochet throw live batting practice and try to return him straight to the Majors as a reliever, potentially for the postseason.
  • "The goal is definitely to get Crochet there, given the potential impact he could make," Ian Browne wrote of the club's thinking, while cautioning that Tanner Houck's path back is less certain this late from Tommy John surgery.
The Final Sprint
  • Boston opens its final 15 games Friday at home against Kansas City (65-82), with Sonny Gray, Ranger Suárez and Payton Tolle lined up for the weekend series.
  • A three-game trip to Texas (Sept. 15-17) and a pivotal set at Tampa Bay (Sept. 18-20) follow, before Cleveland visits Fenway Sept. 22-24 and the Cubs close the regular season Sept. 25-27.
  • Nine of the final 15 games are at Fenway, though Boston has actually been the better road team all season: 44-31 (.587) away from home compared to 36-36 (.500) at Fenway.
Playful Nuggets
  • TWO SKIDDING: Boston's current two-game losing streak is its longest since an Aug. 8-12 stretch (also five games, tied with a season-opening slide as the longest of the year); Friday's opener against Kansas City is a chance to snap it.
  • 400 AND COUNTING: Aroldis Chapman's Sept. 6 save was career No. 400, making him the ninth pitcher in MLB history to reach the milestone.
  • ROAD WARRIORS: Per Baseball-Reference's official home/road split, Boston is 44-31 (.587) away from Fenway this season but just 36-36 (.500) at home — an unusual profile for a club typically built around its ballpark.
  • THE 5-RUN RECIPE, STILL HOLDS: The Red Sox are 57-11 (.838) this season when scoring at least five runs, but just 23-56 (.291) when held under five — exactly what happened in Wednesday's 6-4 loss (Boston scored four).
Odds & Ends
  • Contreras eligible, but idle: Willson Contreras was first eligible to come off the 10-day IL today, but with Boston off, his activation waits until Friday's series opener against Kansas City at the earliest.
  • Mead's timeline in doubt: Curtis Mead has progressed to playing catch but still isn't cleared to swing because of lingering left wrist discomfort; Ian Browne wrote Wednesday he's "not sure he has enough time" to contribute again in 2026.
  • Arias likely to wait: Top position-player prospect Franklin Arias isn't expected to be called up with only 15 games left and Trevor Story holding down shortstop, backed up by Nick Sogard and Andruw Monasterio.
  • Houck's next step: Tanner Houck's second rehab outing is set for Friday at Double-A Portland, with his pitch count expected to jump to 40-45 as he builds toward a return around Sept. 18.
