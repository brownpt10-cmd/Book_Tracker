BOSTON RED SOX (73-60, 3rd AL East, 6.0 GB) — OFF DAY

No game today. Boston is off after Wednesday's series finale in Miami and travels to New York for a four-game weekend set at Yankee Stadium, beginning Friday at 7:15 p.m. ET. Probables: LHP Patrick Sandoval (1-2, 4.54 ERA) for Boston vs. RHP Cam Schlittler (11-6, 2.16 ERA) for New York.
Series stakes: Boston (73-60) sits 2.5 games behind the Yankees (75-57) for the top AL wild card and 6.0 back of division-leading Tampa Bay. A four-game sweep by either club would swing the standings dramatically; New York holds a slim 5-4 season-series edge... er, Boston holds it 5-4, with two wins this weekend clinching the season series outright.
Schedule shape: Friday's opener (7:15 p.m.) is followed by a split-admission doubleheader Saturday — a makeup of the June 6 rainout — at 1:05 p.m. and 7:15 p.m., then a getaway matinee Sunday at 1:35 p.m. Boston has not named a starter for Saturday night; Brayan Bello could open behind bulk relief. New York's Saturday-night plans hinge on whether Max Fried (elbow bone bruise) clears his final tests to return from the 15-day IL.

State of the Sox
  • Boston is 73-60, third in the AL East, 6.0 games behind Tampa Bay (79-54) and 2.5 behind the Yankees (75-57), who sit second. The Red Sox hold the second AL wild card at +7.5 over the cutoff line, with New York +10.0 in front for the top spot.
  • Wednesday's five-game winning streak ended with a thud: a 4-0 shutout loss in Miami in which Boston committed four errors, was held to five hits, went 0-for-4 with runners in scoring position and stranded nine baserunners. Still, the Red Sox took two of three in the series and have won 7 of their last 10.
  • Run differential sits at +101 (600 scored, 499 allowed) through 133 games, just percentage points behind New York's AL-best +104 — the two rosters are about as evenly matched as the standings suggest.
  • Today is a scheduled off day and travel day. The Red Sox next play Friday at 7:15 p.m. ET, opening a four-game set at Yankee Stadium that includes a split-admission doubleheader Saturday (a June 6 rainout makeup) and a Sunday matinee.
  • Twenty-nine games remain, and the next 11 are exclusively road games against the top of the American League: four at New York, three at Baltimore behind a stop in the schedule, and more division ball to come before Boston sees Fenway again.
A NIGHTMARE FINALE:
  • Boston's tidy road trip unraveled all at once Wednesday. Ryan Gusto, who entered 0-3 with a 4.37 ERA, no-hit–quality-lined the Red Sox for 5⅔ scoreless innings to pick up his first win since July 30, 2025. Sonny Gray took the loss despite five innings of two-run, seven-hit ball, falling to 16-4 with his ERA ticking up to 2.82.
  • The defense did the rest of the damage: Connor Wong and Nick Sogard were each charged with two errors, and the Marlins stole six bases without a single caught-stealing. Sogard, who entered the night with just one error all season, tripled that total before the game ended — his fielding error and ensuing throwing error on the same play let Miami's third run score in the eighth.
  • Boston's best chance came in the second, loading the bases with two outs before Sogard grounded out to strand it. NESN's recap says the Red Sox failed to score for the first time since Aug. 5; the club's own primary day-by-day log shows an earlier shutout loss at Toronto on June 17 (0-3) that the recap doesn't account for — a discrepancy flagged rather than resolved here.
  • One more wrinkle: Fox Sports' pitch-by-pitch log has Wyatt Olds relieving Jovani Morán to open the eighth and absorbing both unearned runs, while NESN's game story credits the runs to Will Vest. Both are presented since neither could be independently confirmed against a Baseball-Reference box score, which returned an empty page at build time.
THE CAVALRY, RIGHT ON SCHEDULE:
  • Roman Anthony said Tuesday he could be ready to return from the 60-day IL as soon as Friday's series opener at Yankee Stadium, capping a rehab stint that included a two-run homer in his Worcester debut. Whether the club shares his optimism is still an open question pending one more evaluation.
  • Trevor Story and Isiah Kiner-Falefa remain on rehab assignment at Worcester; both are described as "close" but the club may prefer they finish out the week in the minors rather than parachute in for a single game before New York.
  • Garrett Whitlock threw a live batting-practice session Wednesday in Miami; the club evaluates him today on the scheduled off day.
  • Adley Rutschman is expected back for the Yankees series after missing Boston's last two games with right elbow irritation — interim manager Chad Tracy has called it precautionary and not an IL-caliber issue.
FARM TO TABLE:
  • Boston fell five spots, from No. 11 to No. 16, in ESPN's updated MLB farm-system rankings, with analyst Kiley McDaniel pegging the system's value at $351.9 million. Without the in-season Adley Rutschman trade alone — which cost Boston nearly $100 million in estimated prospect value — McDaniel estimated the Red Sox would rank roughly 10th.
  • The system isn't cratering: Boston still has one of the sport's best individual prospects. Franklin Arias has climbed to No. 2 overall in ESPN's prospect rankings after hitting .375/.432/.764 with 8 home runs in 20 Double-A Portland games.
  • The bigger picture: Payton Tolle and Jake Bennett graduated off the prospect list into the rotation, and Connelly Early did the same before being traded. It's the natural cost of a rebuild that actually produced a contender.
FROM 14 UNDER TO IN THE HUNT:
  • It's worth restating just how far this club has come. After the Yankees swept a three-game set at Fenway in late April (4-0, 4-1, 4-2), Boston sat 9-16 — the front office's day-by-day log confirms game 25 landed the club there. New York returned the favor in June, getting swept four straight at Fenway, punctuated by a three-run ninth-inning comeback and a Jarren Duran walk-off single.
  • The season series sits 5-4 Boston entering this weekend's four-game set, with two Red Sox wins enough to lock the tiebreaker season-series edge before the Wild Card round is even a discussion.
Odds & Ends
  • Quiet transaction ledger: Boston has made no roster moves since Aug. 17 (Wyatt Olds recalled, Raymond Burgos optioned) — the longest stretch of roster stability since before the trade deadline.
  • Yankees' own headaches: New York is also nursing injuries into this series — Giancarlo Stanton (calf) is done for the year and Ryan Weathers (elbow flexor strain) is out for weeks, though both Cody Bellinger and, possibly, Max Fried are trending back toward the active roster.
  • Run differential race: Boston's +101 and New York's +104 are the two best marks in the AL East — both clubs have outplayed a fair number of teams with better raw records this season.
  • Contreras watch: Willson Contreras was not among Wednesday's featured performers; his line is carried forward from Monday's game pending a confirmed update.
