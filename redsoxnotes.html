Cameron, a rookie left-hander, has been a bright spot in an otherwise lost Kansas City season with a 3.93 ERA and 145 strikeouts.
State of the Sox
  • Boston is 81-68, third in the AL East, 8.5 games behind Tampa Bay (89-59), which clinched a playoff berth on Sept. 11.
  • The Red Sox hold the second AL Wild Card with a 6.0-game cushion over Cleveland, which occupies the third and final spot. Boston's magic number to clinch at least a playoff berth is 7 — any combination of Red Sox wins and Toronto losses (Toronto is the first team currently outside the field).
  • Boston is 6-4 over its last 10 games and snapped a three-game losing streak Saturday with a 5-1 win over Kansas City.
  • Thirteen games remain, including a decisive road trip to Tampa Bay (Sept. 18-20) and a season-ending homestand against Cleveland and the Cubs.
Suárez, Abreu End the Skid
  • Ranger Suárez struck out eight over seven innings of one-run ball Saturday, improving to 7-4, as Boston beat Kansas City 5-1 to snap a three-game losing streak.
  • Wilyer Abreu delivered a tiebreaking two-run double, Nick Sogard added an RBI double, and Adley Rutschman and Caleb Durbin each had a run-scoring single.
  • Carter Jensen hit his 24th homer of the season for Kansas City, which had won the series opener Friday. Suárez didn't walk a batter and allowed just five hits.
Contreras Back, Seigler Sidelined With Wrist Injury
  • Willson Contreras was activated from the 10-day IL this morning, a little more than two weeks after straining his left hamstring on Aug. 31; he hit .274/.384/.516 with a team-leading 26 homers in 517 plate appearances before the injury and slots back in at first base and in the middle of the order today.
  • Anthony Seigler was placed on the 10-day IL in the corresponding move with right wrist inflammation. Seigler, who had only been recalled from Worcester on Friday, fouled a pitch off his wrist in the sixth inning Saturday; he finished the at-bat before Isiah Kiner-Falefa moved over to second base in the seventh.
  • Nick Sogard shifts back into the everyday second-base mix with Seigler out; Sogard starts there today.
Fenway Finally Breaks Even
  • Saturday's win pushed Boston's home record to 37-37 (.500) — the first time all season the Red Sox have been at or above .500 at Fenway Park.
  • Boston remains a markedly better road team, at 44-31 (.587) away from home, continuing a reverse split that has held up most of the season.
  • Seven of Boston's 13 remaining games are at Fenway (today's finale plus three each against Cleveland and the Cubs); the other six are on the road against Texas and Tampa Bay.
The Final Sprint
  • After today, Boston plays three at Texas (Sept. 15-17), three at Tampa Bay (Sept. 18-20), three at home against Cleveland (Sept. 22-24), and closes the regular season with three at home against the Cubs (Sept. 25-27).
  • This weekend's Gray-Suárez-Tolle rotation turn against Kansas City looks like a dry run for Boston's likely three-man Wild Card round rotation, per Heavy.com — with Garrett Crochet, last October's Game 1 starter, ticketed for the bullpen at best after his lat injury.
  • Boston lost its 2025 Wild Card series to the Yankees two games to one; a rematch remains the likeliest October scenario if both teams hold their current spots.
Playful Nuggets
  • GRAY STANDS ALONE: Sonny Gray's 17 wins remain the most in the majors, and he's made all 27 of his starts this year without an injured-list trip.
  • MAGIC NUMBER 7: Boston can clinch at least a playoff spot with any combination of seven Red Sox wins and Toronto losses over the final 13 games.
  • WALK-OFF WARRIORS: The Red Sox are 5-3 in walk-off games this season, part of a 36-comeback-win campaign (largest comeback: four runs).
  • EXTRA, EXTRA: Boston is 7-4 (.636) in extra-inning games this year, noticeably better than its .544 overall winning percentage.
Odds & Ends
  • Rafaela eyeing Tampa: The All-Star center fielder says he plans to ramp up running during the Texas trip and, if there are no setbacks, be activated for the Sept. 18 series opener at the Rays — nine games before the playoffs.
  • Crochet's bullpen build-up continues: Friday's session with sweepers "looked good," per Tracy, with another throwing session possible as soon as Tuesday; no firm return date.
  • Houck on schedule: His 40-45 pitch rehab outing Sept. 11 at Double-A Portland keeps him on track for a return to Boston around Sept. 18.
  • Arias, Campbell still long shots: Both remain hot at Triple-A Worcester, but with 13 games left and the roster set, a September look for either appears unlikely.
