State of the Sox
  • Boston is 79-65, third in the AL East, 6.5 games behind Tampa Bay (85-58) and 4.0 behind the Yankees (81-62).

  • Eighteen games remain on the regular-season schedule, including a season-defining set at Tampa Bay (Sep. 18-20) and a season finale against the Cubs at Fenway.
CHAPMAN JOINS THE 400-SAVE CLUB:
  • Aroldis Chapman recorded career save No. 400 in Sunday's series-clinching 3-1 win over Baltimore, becoming the ninth pitcher in major league history to reach the milestone, per MLB.com.
  • The save lowered his ERA to 2.03 with 33 saves on the season, and came a day after Sonny Gray moved into sole possession of the major league lead in wins — giving Boston's late-inning duo a share of the September spotlight.
  • At 38, Chapman remains one of the most reliable closers in the league down the stretch, a key piece as Boston tries to lock up its Wild Card position.
TOLLE'S CAREER NIGHT SEALS THE SWEEP:
  • Payton Tolle struck out a career-high 12 batters over six innings Sunday, allowing just one run, to clinch a four-game sweep of Baltimore.
  • The 23-year-old lefty improved to 9-6 with a 3.15 ERA in his first full big-league season; his 159 strikeouts now rank among the rotation's leaders.
  • Eli White supplied the offensive punch with a three-run homer, filling in admirably in center field with Ceddanne Rafaela sidelined.
HOMESTAND OPENS AGAINST TWO OF THE AL'S WEAKEST CLUBS:
  • Boston opens a six-game homestand today against the Angels (54-89, last in the AL West) and Royals (64-80), two of the American League's worst records, before a far tougher slate begins Sep. 15 at Texas.
  • At 79-65 with 18 games left, the Red Sox hold the inside track on the second AL Wild Card spot; a strong week at Fenway would go a long way toward locking up a return trip to October.
  • Interim manager Chad Tracy's health-first approach for September continues, with an eye toward having a fully healthy roster in place for the stretch run.
PLAYFUL NUGGETS
  • THE 5-RUN RECIPE: Boston is a sparkling 56-11 (.836) this season when scoring at least five runs, but a middling 23-54 (.299) when held under five — a split that explains why the low-scoring, pitching-driven wins in Baltimore (1-0, 5-0, 3-1) mattered so much.
  • SHUTOUT CITY: The Red Sox have thrown 16 shutouts this season against 7 suffered, including two of their last three wins in Baltimore.
  • EXTRA CREDIT: Boston is 5-4 in extra-inning games this year, with 4 walk-off wins against 2 walk-off losses.
  • JULY'S JOYRIDE: The Red Sox' longest winning streak of 2026 remains a 15-game run from July 3-22 that turned a 38-48 start into a 52-48 club — still their single best stretch of the season.
Odds & Ends
