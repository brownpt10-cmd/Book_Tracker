State of the Sox
  • Boston is 73-60, third in the AL East, 6.0 games behind Tampa Bay (79-54, idle Thursday) and now 4.0 behind the Yankees (75-58), who lost to Houston 5-1 Thursday. The Red Sox hold the second AL wild card at +7.5, with New York's cushion for the top spot trimmed to roughly +9.5 after the loss.
  • Boston is idle since Wednesday's 0-4 shutout loss in Miami, which snapped a five-game winning streak; the Red Sox are 7-3 in their last 10 and 9 of their last 13 overall, per NESN's series-preview framing tonight.
  • Run differential sits at +101 (600 scored, 499 allowed) through 133 games, still one of the two best marks in the AL East alongside New York's.
  • Twenty-nine games remain, and the next 11 are exclusively road games against the top of the American League: four at New York, then more of the schedule before Boston returns to Fenway.
  • A Boston win tonight, paired with New York's Thursday loss, would pull the Red Sox to within 3.0 games of the division lead's toughest competitor and set up a chance to erase the wild-card gap over the weekend.
LAST DANCE AT THE STADIUM:
  • Tonight opens the final regular-season series between Boston and New York this year — four games in three days, capped by a split-admission doubleheader Saturday that makes up the June 6 rainout. Whatever happens over these four games is the last word on the season series before October positioning takes over.
  • Boston already won the four-game series at Fenway June 25-28 by a combined 21-9, the start of a 41-14 stretch that turned a 14-games-under-.500 club into a wild-card contender. New York has not forgotten: Schlittler's own April start against Boston was part of an earlier Yankees sweep at Fenway.
  • The season series sits 5-4 Boston. Split the results of these four games and the Red Sox lock the tiebreaker outright with a win Saturday or Sunday; get swept and New York claims it back.
ANTHONY WAITS, THE REST OF THE CAVALRY WATCHES:
  • Roman Anthony will not join the team for tonight's opener. The Worcester Telegram's Tommy Cassell reported Friday that Anthony is still at Polar Park as Boston heads to the Bronx; neither the outfielder nor the club had ever set a hard target of tonight. Anthony went 2-for-4 with an RBI double and single in Thursday night's rehab game and is batting .304 across his Portland-and-Worcester rehab stops with two home runs, though he said postgame he doesn't feel fully back at the plate yet.
  • Trevor Story (4-for-21, five strikeouts through his rehab games) and Isiah Kiner-Falefa (1-for-13, four strikeouts) remain at Worcester; both have struggled at the plate in the minors, giving the club less urgency to rush either one up the middle of an infield currently manned capably by Andruw Monasterio and Nick Sogard.
  • Garrett Whitlock threw a live BP session Wednesday in Miami and struck out both hitters he faced; as of Friday's build there is no confirmed word he has been activated off the 15-day IL, with the decision hinging on how his elbow responded.
  • Adley Rutschman is back in the lineup tonight after sitting out Boston's final two games in Miami with right elbow irritation — not an IL-caliber issue, per interim manager Chad Tracy.
MICKEY JONES, EVERYWHERE:
  • With Anthony, Story, Kiner-Falefa and now Yoshida all out at once, Boston has leaned on the improbable tandem of Mickey Gasper and Jahmai Jones — nicknamed "Mickey Jones" in the clubhouse. Gasper is hitting .417/.576/1.167 in August with five home runs and 12 RBIs in just 24 at-bats; his full-season line sits at .275/.364/.466 (.830 OPS) over 43 games.
  • Jones, acquired from Detroit on July 14, has been even hotter in his small sample: .500/.581/.962 (1.542 OPS) in August, with a .768 OPS against left-handed pitching specifically. His full-season Boston line is .500/.581/.962 through 19 games and 26 at-bats.
  • Gasper starts at DH tonight against Schlittler; both he and Jones figure to lose at-bats once the rehabbing trio is fully healthy, which is part of why the front office isn't rushing anyone back.
THE ARIAS WATCH:
  • Boston's consensus No. 1 prospect, 20-year-old infielder Franklin Arias, is slashing .301/.368/.495 (.863 OPS) with four home runs and 24 RBIs in 24 Triple-A Worcester games since a late-July promotion — on top of a .318/.407/.587 line with 19 homers in 75 games at Double-A Portland. He has 23 total home runs in 99 pro games this year, easily a career high.
  • WooSox manager Iggy Suarez said this week that Arias has been studying Story and Kiner-Falefa up close during their rehab stints, and that the ball sounds "different, a little louder" coming off Arias's bat — the same phrase scouts have used for Roman Anthony.
  • No call-up is imminent, but with rosters expanding Sept. 1 and Boston's middle infield picture unsettled, Arias is squarely part of the September conversation.
Odds & Ends
  • Transaction ledger: Boston signed veteran catcher Tomas Nido to a minor-league contract Thursday, adding organizational catching depth at Worcester while Rutschman's elbow is managed carefully; he is not on the 40-man and isn't expected to join the big-league roster soon.
  • Yankees' own headaches: New York lost to Houston 5-1 Thursday, with shortstop George Lombard Jr. charged with an error for a sixth straight game. Giancarlo Stanton (calf) is done for the year and Ryan Weathers (elbow flexor strain) is out for weeks, though Max Fried is expected back this weekend.
  • Run differential race: Boston's +101 and New York's still-AL-East-best mark remain the two top marks in the division — both clubs continue to outplay their raw records by advanced measures.
  • Rosters expand Monday: Sept. 1 brings active rosters from 26 to 28 players, which could ease the roster crunch complicating Anthony's, Story's and Kiner-Falefa's returns.
