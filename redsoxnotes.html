Two pitchers arriving at the same ERA from opposite directions. Alcántara has thrown 63 more innings than Suárez and has been the better pitcher lately by a distance: 3-3 with a 1.96 ERA over his last seven starts, 46 innings, with six or seven innings in every one of them. He has also handled Boston — three career starts, 19 innings, a 1.42 ERA, most recently a no-decision at Fenway in August 2025. Suárez brings the counter-history: 3-0 with a 3.00 ERA and 31 strikeouts in 30 innings across five career starts against Miami. His 2.87 FIP is the best among Boston starters and he has surrendered only seven home runs all year, which matters in a ballpark that suppresses them. He turns 31 on Wednesday. Suárez won his last time out, Aug. 18 against Arizona, despite giving up four runs in 5 1/3 innings.
State of the Sox
  • Boston is 71-59 after Sunday's 5-4 win completed a three-game sweep of San Francisco. That is third in the AL East, 6.0 games behind Tampa Bay (77-53) with the Yankees (74-56) in between, and a season-best-matching three-game winning streak.
  • The wild card remains the honest path. Boston holds the second AL wild card and is 6.5 games clear of the final spot, which is now a three-way tie at 65-66 among Cleveland, Houston and Texas. Toronto is 1.5 back of that group, Minnesota and Baltimore 2.0.
  • Run differential is +99 (589 scored, 490 allowed) in 130 games. Baseball-Reference's Pythagorean record is 76-54, five wins better than the actual mark — still the largest such gap in the division.
  • Thirty-two games left, 17 of them on the road. Eleven are against AL East opponents and every one is away from Fenway: four at Yankee Stadium, four at Camden Yards, three at Tampa Bay. Boston is finished with Toronto.
  • The schedule also hands Boston its own tiebreakers. Three at Texas (Sept. 15-17) and three at home against Cleveland (Sept. 22-24) come against two of the three clubs currently tied for the last wild card.
TWENTY-SEVEN AND THREE:
  • The number that explains the season: from June 27 through Aug. 7 — games 86 through 115 — the Red Sox went 27-3. MLB.com calls it a franchise record for a 30-game stretch. Boston entered that run 37-48 and left it 64-51.
  • Then came the correction: 2-7 over the next nine, including a four-game slide in Toronto that pushed the deficit from 5.0 to 10.0 games.
  • And now the answer to the correction: 5-1 on the homestand just completed, two of three from Arizona and a sweep of the Giants. Seven wins in the last 10, three in a row, and the East deficit back down to 6.0.
THE TRACY NUMBER:
  • Chad Tracy took over on April 26 with the Red Sox 10-17. They are 61-42 since. That is a .592 clip over 103 games from a bench coach who had never managed in the majors.
  • Boston is reportedly leaning toward removing the interim tag, though the club intends to wait until after the season to decide. Tracy is already under contract through 2027, which is why waiting costs nothing.
  • Ron Washington, who coached Tracy as a player, spent part of the weekend saying publicly what the standings say quietly.
A CLAUSE, NOT A PITCH:
  • Sunday was Garrett Crochet's 120th consecutive day on the injured list — and that number, not any pitch he threw, changed his contract. The threshold triggered a provision in his six-year, $170 million extension.
  • The result: Crochet's opt-out after the 2030 season is voided, and a $15 million club option for 2032 has been added. Boston gains a potential extra year of control over the 2025 AL Cy Young runner-up.
  • The clause covered any left-arm injury between 2026 and 2031. Crochet made six starts with a 6.30 ERA before landing on the IL on April 29, retroactive to April 26 — the same day Tracy took over the club.
THE CAVALRY ON THE TURNPIKE:
  • Roman Anthony finished a four-game stint at Double-A Portland on Sunday and moves up to Triple-A Worcester on Tuesday. He went 3-for-11 there with a double, a long home run and two walks. Tracy would not rule out Miami but pointed toward New York.
  • Trevor Story and Isiah Kiner-Falefa were both examined by team doctors at Fenway today. Story is 4-for-21 in six rehab games at Worcester; Kiner-Falefa is 1-for-13. Tracy called their availability for this series "up in the air."
  • Garrett Whitlock threw his first bullpen since going on the IL on Sunday and could rejoin the club during this trip. Justin Slaten played catch for the first time in a week. Tanner Houck throws a second live batting practice today.
THE 5-RUN RECIPE:
  • Score five or more and the Red Sox are 51-10 (.836). Hold them under five and they are 20-49 (.290). Sunday was the 51st — five runs exactly, the smallest qualifying number.
  • The rest of the ledger is less flattering: 15-16 in one-run games and 5-4 in extra innings. Those two lines are the entire explanation for the five-game gap between the real record and the Pythagorean one.
ROAD SWEET ROAD, AND A LANDLORD WHO DOESN'T SHARE:
  • Boston is 37-27 away from Fenway and 34-32 at home — still one of the very few contenders whose road record is the better one. Seventeen of the last 32 games are on the road, which for this club is not obviously bad news.
  • Miami, though, is 41-25 at loanDepot park and 26-39 everywhere else. A 15-game home/road swing is enormous for a .511 team.
  • The counterweight: the Marlins are 14-19 against left-handed starters, and Boston starts left-handers in two of the three games here. The Red Sox themselves are 20-12 when the opposing starter is a lefty and 51-47 when he is right-handed, as Alcántara is tonight.
Odds & Ends
  • The 452: Statcast finally settled the argument about Willson Contreras' Friday home run — 116 mph off the bat, a 30-degree launch angle, 452 feet, on an 82-mph bat speed. It cleared everything in left field.
  • The .520 man: Jahmai Jones is 13-for-25 since being recalled — .520/.586/1.000 in 17 games, with a 340 OPS+. Twenty-five at-bats is nothing and the number is still absurd.
  • Sogard's second: Nick Sogard's 415-foot drive over the Monster on Sunday was the second home run of his major-league career and started the comeback from 3-0 down. He is on base at a .401 clip in 35 games.
  • Rafaela, quietly: Three hits Sunday, two RBIs and his 17th steal. He leads the team in WAR (5.8) and hits (143) and has played 124 of his 125 games in center field.
  • On this date: The Red Sox are 57-61 all-time on Aug. 24. No player on the 40-man roster was born today; Ranger Suárez, tonight's starter, turns 31 on Wednesday, the day the club leaves Miami.

ROTATION & BULLPEN NOTES

  • Ranger Suárez opens the Miami series tonight. He is 5-3 with a 3.41 ERA, and his 2.87 FIP and seven home runs allowed are both the best marks among Boston starters. He is 3-0 with a 3.00 ERA in five career starts against the Marlins.
  • The rest of the trip lines up Payton Tolle and Sonny Gray in Miami, then Jake Bennett and Patrick Sandoval to open the four-game set at Yankee Stadium. Note that Ian Browne's Sunday report had Tolle drawing Sandy Alcántara on Tuesday; the official probables now put Alcántara opposite Suárez tonight.
  • Bennett closed the homestand with his best start in six weeks: seven innings on nine days' rest, six strikeouts, one walk, four runs (three earned) on seven hits, all of the damage in the first two innings. It was his first seven-inning outing since July 8, and the extra rest was by design as Boston manages a career-high professional workload.
  • Gray is 16-3 with a 2.79 ERA through 24 starts and leads the majors in wins at age 36. He closes the Miami series Wednesday with a 3.42 strikeout-to-walk ratio and 15 home runs allowed in 563 batters faced.
  • Tolle leads the team with 134 strikeouts at 23 years old and carries a 1.083 WHIP. He has not allowed more than three earned runs in a start since July.
  • The bullpen has been the season's steadiest asset. Aroldis Chapman closed Sunday for his 29th save and owns a 1.85 ERA at 38; Boston's relief corps entered the week with the lowest ERA in the majors despite losing Garrett Whitlock and Justin Slaten this month.
  • Help may arrive on this trip. Whitlock threw his first bullpen since the IL stint on Sunday and could be activated in Miami or New York. Slaten played catch Sunday for the first time in a week and is targeting September.
  • Tanner Houck throws a second live batting practice today and may not be far from a rehab assignment. Garrett Crochet is aiming to return as a reliever; his 120th day on the IL on Sunday triggered the contract clause described on page 1.
  • Brayan Bello remains the bulk arm behind an opener, a role that has produced a 4-1 record since he moved out of the rotation. Alec Gamboa (1.40 ERA, 297 ERA+) is the opener of choice.

LINEUP NOTES

  • Tonight's order is not out yet. Against a right-hander, the recent template has been Duran or Rafaela at the top, Contreras in the middle and a catcher — Wong or Rutschman — hitting eighth or ninth.
  • Willson Contreras is still the engine: .282/.392/.533 with 26 home runs, 78 RBIs and a 160 OPS+, comfortably the best line on the club. He leads the team in homers, RBIs, on-base and slugging.
  • Ceddanne Rafaela leads Boston position players in WAR at 5.8, hitting .289 with 16 home runs, 17 steals, a team-high 143 hits and 124 games in center field. Wilyer Abreu is next at 5.3 with 22 homers from right.

  • Jarren Duran's season remains the outlier: .207/.265/.362 with 17 home runs and 18 steals in 122 games, a 76 OPS+. His glove in left has kept him playing most days.
  • Jahmai Jones is 13-for-25 since being recalled — a .520 average and a 340 OPS+ in 17 games. Twenty-five at-bats is a sample size, not a season, and it should be read that way.
  • The group gets deeper this week. Trevor Story and Isiah Kiner-Falefa were examined at Fenway today after rehab stints at Worcester; Roman Anthony reports to Worcester on Tuesday. Chad Tracy called this series "up in the air" for the two infielders.
  • Masataka Yoshida's outlook is the discouraging one. An Aug. 17 MRI showed a significant left hamstring strain; he is seeking additional opinions and may be running out of regular season.
