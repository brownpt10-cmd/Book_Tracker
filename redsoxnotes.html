Two left-handed rookies, and almost nothing in common beyond that. Bennett, 25, makes his 15th start on eight days' rest — the Red Sox skipped his turn to protect a career-high professional innings load, and he has not pitched since Aug. 14 at Pittsburgh. His 1.066 WHIP and 1.9 walks per nine are the best marks of any Boston starter. Wilkinson, 23, makes his second big-league appearance and his second start: he threw two scoreless innings against Cleveland — the organization that traded him to San Francisco for Patrick Bailey in May — on Aug. 19, retiring six of the seven men he faced and topping out at 91 mph with the rising four-seamer scouts have nicknamed the "Invisiball." Boston is 19-12 against left-handed starters this season; San Francisco is 15-21. Wilkinson's WHIP is omitted above because a two-inning sample does not mean anything yet.
State of the Sox
  • Boston is 70-59, third in the AL East and 6.0 games behind Tampa Bay (76-53) with the Yankees (73-56) in between. Both wins this weekend came against a club that has lost 10 of 13, and both cut a half-game off the deficit.
  • The Wild Card is the live path. Boston holds the second AL wild card and is 6.5 games clear of the final spot, which Cleveland and Texas share at 64-66. Toronto is a half-game behind that pair, Minnesota and Baltimore a full game.
  • Run differential is +98 (584 scored, 486 allowed) across 129 games. Baseball-Reference's Pythagorean record puts Boston at 75-54 — five wins better than the actual mark, the largest such gap of any AL East club.
  • A win this afternoon completes Boston's first three-game sweep since Aug. 4-6 against the White Sox, and its 10th series sweep of at least three games this season.
  • Thirty-three games remain. Eleven are against AL East opponents — four at Yankee Stadium, four at Camden Yards, three at Tampa Bay — and every one of them is on the road. Boston is finished with Toronto for the year.
THIRTY-THREE OF FORTY-FOUR:
  • Since July 3, the Red Sox are 33-11. That is the run that turned a 39-48 team into a playoff position, and it has not slowed: 6-4 over the last 10, 12-8 over the last 20, 19-11 over the last 30.
  • Since the All-Star break the Sox are 24-11. They were 46-48 when play resumed July 17 and have gained 13 games on .500 in 35 tries.
THE COMEBACK BUSINESS:
  • Saturday's three-run ninth was Boston's 31st comeback win and fourth walk-off of the season. It also finished a nine-inning game in which the Red Sox trailed entering the last frame — the kind of game they lost routinely in April and have made a habit of stealing since June.
  • The mechanics were entirely unglamorous: a walk, a hit batsman, a fielder's choice, an Eli White single, a Nick Sogard double, an intentional walk, and a Wilyer Abreu grounder that beat a throw home by inches after a crew-chief review. Abreu was 0-for-4 with four strikeouts before the at-bat that won it.
  • Boston is 14-16 in one-run games and 5-4 in extra innings — the underwhelming half of the ledger, and the reason the Pythagorean record sits five wins ahead of the real one.
THE 5-RUN RECIPE, BROKEN:
  • Score five or more and the Red Sox are 50-10 (.833). Hold them under five and they are 20-49 (.290). It has been the least subtle split on the team since May.
  • Saturday was the 20th of those 69 wins — and only the sixth of them since the All-Star break. Boston managed six hits, no extra-base hit until Mickey Gasper's eighth-inning double, and loaded the bases with nobody out in the eighth without scoring.
WAKEFIELD'S COMPANY:
  • Sonny Gray's Friday win made him 16-3 through 24 starts. In the last 50 years the only other Red Sox pitcher to win at least 16 of his first 24 starts with the club is Tim Wakefield in 1995, who went 16-5 with a 2.71 ERA in his knuckleballing debut summer in Boston.
  • Gray leads the majors in wins at age 36. His ERA sits at 2.79 with a 123-to-36 strikeout-to-walk ratio, and he closes the Miami series Wednesday.
FIRST OF MANY:
  • Wyatt Olds was selected from Triple-A Worcester on Aug. 17. Five days later he recorded four outs across the eighth and ninth innings and walked off the mound with a 1-0 record — his first big-league win, in his third big-league appearance, without having allowed a run in any of them.
  • The 26-year-old right-hander has faced 13 hitters as a major leaguer: two hits, two walks, four strikeouts, zero runs. He is one of eight pitchers to make his major-league debut in a Boston uniform this season.
HOME AND AWAY, EVENED OUT:
  • Boston is 33-32 at Fenway Park and 37-27 away from it. The two wins this weekend nudged the home record above .500; for most of the summer the Red Sox have been the only AL East club with a losing home record and a winning road one.
  • The split by opponent is stranger still: 17-8 against the AL Central, 22-19 in interleague play, 12-10 against the AL West — and 19-22 inside the division. The AL East record is the entire reason Boston is chasing rather than leading.
Odds & Ends
  • Contreras, in orbit: Willson Contreras' three-run homer off Logan Webb on Friday was measured at roughly 450 feet over the Green Monster, his 26th of the year. He is hitting .285/.395/.538 with a 162 OPS+, the best offensive line on the team.
  • Sogard on base: Nick Sogard reached four times Saturday — single, double, two walks — matching a career high and extending his hitting streak to five games. He is on base at a .392 clip in 34 games.
  • Bullpen, still first: Boston entered the weekend with the best bullpen ERA in the majors at 2.94, and the relief corps has not allowed a run in either game of this series — 6 1/3 innings across Friday and Saturday.
  • Nobody's birthday: No player on the 40-man roster was born on Aug. 23. Bench coach Ramón Vázquez turned 50 on Friday.

ROTATION & BULLPEN NOTES

  • Jake Bennett closes the homestand this afternoon on eight days' rest. Boston skipped his turn to hold down a career-high professional innings total; he last pitched Aug. 14 at Pittsburgh.
  • The Miami rotation is set: Ranger Suárez Monday, Payton Tolle Tuesday against Sandy Alcántara, Sonny Gray Wednesday. That leaves Bennett and Patrick Sandoval to open the four-game set at Yankee Stadium beginning Friday.
  • Gray is 16-3 with a 2.79 ERA through 24 starts, leading the majors in wins at 36. He allowed four runs in six innings Friday — a two-run Drew Gilbert homer and a Rafael Devers solo shot — and still earned the decision.
  • Sandoval's Saturday line (5.2 IP, 5 H, 2 R, 2 BB, 6 SO, 93 pitches) was the steadying performance Boston needed after his seven-run outing at Pittsburgh. He has allowed two earned runs or fewer in six of his eight starts as a Red Sox.
  • The bullpen has not allowed a run in this series: Greg Weissert, Jovani Morán and Wyatt Olds combined for 3 1/3 scoreless innings Saturday after Aroldis Chapman closed out Friday with three strikeouts. Boston's 2.94 relief ERA is the best in the majors.
  • Help may be close. Garrett Whitlock reached 120 feet in his throwing program on Aug. 21 and could be activated at or near the Aug. 25 expiration of his 15-day stint. Justin Slaten's MRI showed no structural damage; September is the target.
  • Tanner Houck throws a second live batting practice Monday and may not be far from a rehab assignment. Garrett Crochet has stretched his throwing to 90 feet and is aiming to return as a reliever, not a starter.
  • Boston has used an opener successfully twice this month, most notably Aug. 17 when Alec Gamboa retired all four men he faced before Brayan Bello covered 4 2/3 innings for the win.
