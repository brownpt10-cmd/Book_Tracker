Baseball-Reference's series preview lists Patrick Sandoval as Boston's Game 1 starter against Texas; the Rangers had not named a probable as of this issue. Sandoval has been the rotation's soft spot this season (1-5, 4.58 ERA) but gets a Rangers lineup that finally climbed back to .500 in mid-June before fading; Texas sits 2nd in the AL West and 2.0 games back in the Wild Card race, so this series carries stakes on both sides. Boston's rotation turned over Gray-Suárez-Tolle against Kansas City Friday-through-Sunday; Sandoval on regular rest slots in as the four-hole starter for this turn.
State of the Sox
  • Boston is 82-68, third in the AL East, 8.5 games behind Tampa Bay (90-59), which has clinched a playoff berth.
  • The Red Sox hold the second AL Wild Card with a 6.0-game cushion over Cleveland, which occupies the third and final spot. Boston's magic number to clinch at least a playoff berth is down to 6 after taking two of three from Kansas City over the weekend — any combination of six Red Sox wins and Toronto losses (Toronto is the current first team out) clinches at least a Wild Card spot.
  • Boston has won two straight and is 7-3 over its last 10 games; the club is 36-20 since the All-Star break.
  • Twelve games remain, including this week's road trip to Texas and Tampa Bay and a season-ending homestand against Cleveland and the Cubs.
Tolle's Immaculate Inning Punctuates a Series Win
  • Payton Tolle opened Sunday's series finale by striking out the side on nine pitches — an immaculate inning, the eighth in Red Sox history and the first since Chris Sale's in 2021. It was also the first time a Red Sox pitcher opened a game with one since Pedro Martínez in 2002, and just the sixth immaculate first inning recorded in MLB history.
  • Tolle went on to throw six shutout innings, allowing four hits and striking out seven on 96 pitches, as Boston beat Kansas City 4-1 to take the final two games of the series. He improved to 10-6; Noah Cameron (six innings, one run allowed) took the loss for Kansas City.
  • Caleb Durbin's 372-foot solo homer — his 13th of the year, snapping an 89-at-bat drought — opened the scoring in the second. Roman Anthony's go-ahead single in the seventh and Trevor Story's two-run single in the eighth (scoring Willson Contreras and Adley Rutschman) finished it off.
  • Aroldis Chapman worked around two walks in the ninth, striking out the side including Bobby Witt Jr. on a 2-2 heater, for his 401st career save — moving him another step closer to Billy Wagner's 422 for eighth on the all-time list.
First to 46, First Above .500
  • Sunday's win officially clinched a winning record for Boston, which is now the first team in Major League history to lose 46 of its first 78 games and still finish a season above .500. The Red Sox were 32-46 in late June.
  • Boston has held opponents to one run or fewer 39 times this season — a league high, and tied with the 1946 Red Sox for the most such games in a season during the Live-Ball Era (since 1920).
  • The turnaround has been fueled by a 36-20 record since the All-Star break, among the best marks in baseball over that stretch.
Off Day, On to Texas
  • Boston travels to Arlington for a three-game set opening Tuesday against a Rangers club (74-76) that is technically alive in the Wild Card race but 2.0 games back with 12 to play.
  • After Texas: three at Tampa Bay (Sept. 18-20, Boston's last shot at the division champion and Ceddanne Rafaela's targeted return series), then a season-ending Fenway homestand against Cleveland (Sept. 22-24, Boston's chief Wild Card rival) and the Cubs (Sept. 25-27).
  • Of the 12 games remaining, six are on the road (Texas, Tampa Bay) and six are at Fenway (Cleveland, Chicago Cubs).
The Return Timeline
  • Ceddanne Rafaela (right quad strain) remains on track for the Sept. 18 series opener at Tampa Bay, which would give him roughly nine games to get up to speed before the postseason; he was eligible to return this week but the club has been in no rush given the schedule.
  • Garrett Crochet (left lat) will skip a minor-league rehab assignment entirely, since Triple-A Worcester's season ends Sept. 20 — instead he's expected to face live hitters in simulated-game work over the final week, with an eye toward a relief role if he's activated for the postseason.
  • Tanner Houck (Tommy John recovery) threw three innings in his second rehab outing Sept. 11 at Double-A Portland and is expected to step up to Triple-A Worcester for his next assignment.
Playful Nuggets
  • IMMACULATE TOLLE: Nine pitches, nine strikes, three strikeouts — Payton Tolle's first-inning immaculate frame Sunday was the eighth in Red Sox history and the first to open a Red Sox game since Pedro Martínez in 2002.
  • THE .500 CLUB: After opening the year 32-46, Boston is the first team in MLB history to lose 46 of its first 78 games and still finish a season with a winning record.
  • LOCKDOWN SEASON: Boston has held an opponent to one run or fewer 39 times in 2026, a league high that ties the 1946 Red Sox for the most such games in a season during the Live-Ball Era.
  • CHAPMAN'S CLIMB: Sunday's save was career No. 401 for Aroldis Chapman, pulling him within 21 of Billy Wagner's 422 for eighth on the all-time saves list.
  • MAGIC NUMBER 6: Boston's magic number to clinch at least a playoff berth sits at 6 with 12 games to play.
Odds & Ends
  • Story's stretch: Trevor Story has hit safely in 12 of his last 13 games, a stretch that has quieted the fans who wanted him off the roster earlier this year.
  • Contreras looks sharp: Back from a 12-game absence, Contreras went 2-for-3 with a walk Sunday and slots right back into the middle of the order.
  • Rotation dry run continues: With Crochet ticketed for the bullpen at best, this week's Sandoval-and-beyond turns in Texas are as much about setting up October length as they are about the standings.
  • Worcester winding down: Triple-A Worcester's season ends Sept. 20, which shapes both Crochet's and Houck's remaining rehab plans.
