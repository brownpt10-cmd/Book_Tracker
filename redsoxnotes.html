This is the best pitching matchup of the weekend and arguably one of the best on the Fenway calendar. Gray, 36, leads the majors in wins and has a 2.72 ERA over his last seven starts, including seven scoreless innings in the 4-0 shutout at Pittsburgh on Aug. 15. Webb's record understates him badly — he has the lower WHIP (1.06) and more innings (139.2), and he has allowed one run or fewer in each of his last three starts for a club that has lost 7 of 10. San Francisco arrives 23-42 on the road and 15-27 in interleague play; Boston is 20-19 against the National League. Webb makes his 23rd start of the year, Gray his 24th.
State of the Sox
  • Boston is 68-59 and third in the AL East, but the gap closed a half-game while the Sox sat: Tampa Bay lost Thursday, trimming the deficit from 8.5 to 8.0. The Rays are 76-51, the Yankees 72-55.
  • The Wild Card position is the one that matters. Boston holds WC2 and is 5.5 games clear of the final spot, currently occupied by Minnesota (63-65) with Texas (63-65) tied alongside. Nine AL clubs are within three games of that cutline; Boston is not one of them.
  • Run differential stands at +95 (575 scored, 480 allowed), computed from the full 127-game day-by-day log on Page 5.
  • Thursday was the club's first scheduled off day since Aug. 3 — 16 games in 16 days across four cities, an 8-8 wash. Tonight opens the back half of a six-game homestand before an eight-game trip through Miami and New York.
  • Boston is 4-6 over its last 10 but 12-8 over its last 20 and 19-11 over its last 30. The recent slide has not moved the standings much because nobody behind them has strung anything together either.
The Devers Game
  • Rafael Devers returns to Fenway Park tonight for the first time since the June 15, 2025 trade to San Francisco — the deal that followed his refusal to move off third base to DH and then to first.
  • Devers has been publicly unbothered about the reception: "I feel very happy going back over there. I love the fans. I'm not concerned."
  • He spent eight-plus seasons in Boston, arriving as a 20-year-old in 2017 and leaving as an All-Star, a Silver Slugger and a World Series champion. Whatever the crowd does with the first at-bat will be the loudest thing that happens at Fenway this month.
  • The second reunion is on hold. Marcelo Mayer, Boston's former top infield prospect, went to San Francisco on Aug. 3 for Erik Miller and Carlos Gutierrez and is still on the IL with a left ulna bone stress reaction. Giants manager Tony Vitello has indicated Mayer is more likely to be activated next week than this weekend.
The Cavalry Slows Down
  • Trevor Story will not be activated for tonight's opener. After Thursday's scheduled recovery day, the shortstop was slated to rejoin Worcester's lineup at Syracuse — a delay from the weekend target that had been floated earlier in the week, though the rehab itself has gone well (2-for-4 with an RBI and six innings at short on Wednesday).
  • Isiah Kiner-Falefa, out since June 20 with a forearm stress fracture, is on his own rehab assignment at Worcester and played seven innings at second base in the same game.
  • Roman Anthony opened a rehab assignment at Double-A Portland on Aug. 18 and homered in his first game back — 2-for-2 with two walks as the DH.
  • Garrett Crochet is the one who is probably not coming back as a starter. Chad Tracy has called a rotation return unlikely, saying there isn't time to build to six-inning outings before the end of September; a multi-inning bullpen role in the final weeks is the realistic version.
TWELVE UNDER TO NINE OVER:
  • On June 10, after a 7-5 loss at Tampa Bay, Boston was 27-39 — twelve games under .500 and 13.5 out. Since then the Red Sox are 41-20 (.672), the third-best 61-game run in the American League over that window by their own log.
  • Since the All-Star break specifically, the Sox are 22-11. They were 46-48 when play resumed on July 17 and have gained 11 games on .500 in 33 tries.
ROAD SWEET ROAD, STILL:
  • Boston is 37-27 (.578) away from Fenway and 31-32 (.492) at home — the only club in the AL East with a losing home record and a winning road one. Tonight begins a stretch of three straight at home against a team that is 23-42 on the road.
  • The Sox are 5-4 in extra innings, 3-3 in walk-off games and 13-16 in one-run games. San Francisco is 4-6 in extras and a dismal 11-21 in one-run games.
THE COMPANY THEY KEEP:
  • Boston is 17-8 against the AL Central, 12-10 against the AL West and 20-19 in interleague play — but just 19-22 inside its own division. The division record is the whole reason the Sox are chasing rather than leading.
  • Twenty-two of Boston's remaining 35 games are against AL East opponents, starting with four at Yankee Stadium next weekend.
THE 5-RUN RECIPE:
  • Score five or more and the Red Sox are 49-10 (.831). Hold them under five and they are 19-49 (.279). It is the least subtle split on the team and it has barely moved since May.
  • Boston has thrown 13 shutouts and been shut out five times — still the widest such margin in the AL East. Gray owns three of the 13.
Odds & Ends
  • Gray's 15: Sonny Gray leads all of baseball in wins at 36 years old and sits fifth in MLB.com's latest AL Cy Young straw poll, the highest any Red Sox pitcher has placed this season.
  • Nine for nine, none for the K column: In Monday's 11-1 win over Arizona, every Boston starter drove in a run and the team never struck out — the first time the Red Sox managed both in one game since June 25, 1949.
  • Mighty Mickey: Mickey Gasper had never homered in a Red Sox uniform before Aug. 17. He then homered on Aug. 17, 18 and 19 — all three of his 2026 home runs on consecutive nights.
  • Bullpen depth thinning: With Garrett Whitlock and Justin Slaten on the 15-day IL and Zack Kelly, Johan Oviedo, Tanner Houck and Kutter Crawford on the 60-day, Boston is running a relief corps built largely from in-season additions. Aroldis Chapman, 38, closes it with a 1.94 ERA and 27 saves.

ROTATION & BULLPEN NOTES

  • Gray goes tonight against Logan Webb in the best pitching matchup of the weekend, then Patrick Sandoval Saturday and Jake Bennett Sunday. Bennett will be working on nine days' rest — the Red Sox deliberately skipped his turn to manage a career-high professional innings total.
  • Gray has been the best version of himself all year: 15-3 with a 2.65 ERA and a 117-to-34 strikeout-to-walk ratio, tops in the majors in wins and fifth in MLB.com's latest AL Cy Young poll. He carries a 2.72 ERA over his last seven starts into tonight, at age 36.
  • Thursday's off day, the first since Aug. 3, resets the whole staff. Every reliever on the active roster is available tonight for the first time in more than two weeks.
  • Boston used a true opener for the first time successfully on Aug. 17, with Alec Gamboa retiring all four batters he faced on 14 pitches before handing off to Brayan Bello for 4 2/3 innings and the win. Bello's second-half bulk-relief work has become one of the staff's quiet strengths.
  • Payton Tolle followed his eight-shutout-inning masterpiece with a rougher Aug. 19 outing, allowing all three of his runs in the fifth; he still owns a 3.08 ERA and a team-high 134 strikeouts.
  • Bullpen attrition continues. Justin Slaten went on the 15-day IL Aug. 16 with elbow inflammation, joining Garrett Whitlock (right elbow inflammation, 15-day). Garrett Crochet, Zack Kelly, Johan Oviedo, Tanner Houck and Kutter Crawford remain on the 60-day IL. Jovani Morán took the loss Wednesday in the 10th.
  • Aroldis Chapman triggered his 2027 vesting option on Aug. 16 and continues to close with a 1.94 ERA and 27 saves at age 38.
