Rosters expand from 26 to 28 players Monday, Sept. 1.

State of the Sox
  • Boston is 73-61, third in the AL East, 7.0 games behind Tampa Bay (80-54) and 3.0 behind the Yankees (76-58) after Friday's 1-0 loss. The Red Sox hold the second AL wild card at roughly +5.0 over Cleveland (68-66, WC3, unconfirmed since Aug. 26 and flagged approximate).
  • Boston has lost two straight and been shut out in both, its longest scoreless streak of the season at 18 innings entering today's doubleheader.
  • Run differential sits at roughly +100 (600 scored, 500 allowed) through 134 games, still among the best marks in the AL East.
  • Twenty-eight games remain, including the rest of this series (a doubleheader today plus Sunday's finale) before a return to Fenway Monday against Seattle.
  • A Boston win in either game today would snap the scoreless streak and put real pressure on New York with two games still to play in the series.
SILENCED IN THE BRONX:
  • Boston has gone 18 consecutive innings without a run, losing 0-4 at Miami Wednesday and 1-0 to the Yankees Friday. Patrick Sandoval's excellent series-opening start (5 1/3 IP, one run allowed — Goldschmidt's solo homer) went to waste as the Red Sox drew six walks but finished 0-for-9 with runners in scoring position and stranded 10 baserunners.
  • Ceddanne Rafaela and Wilyer Abreu combined for four of Boston's five hits Friday; the rest of the lineup went a combined 1-for-24, including hitless nights from Contreras, Rutschman, Duran and Sogard.
  • Chad Tracy responded with a shaken-up Game 1 lineup: Jahmai Jones moves to leadoff and DH, Eli White takes over left field, and Jarren Duran is out of the starting nine entirely for the day game.
WHITLOCK'S BACK, OLDS OPTIONED:
  • Garrett Whitlock was reinstated from the 15-day injured list (right elbow inflammation) Saturday morning, with Wyatt Olds optioned to Worcester as the corresponding move. Boston also recalled Raymond Burgos as the 27th player permitted for today's doubleheader, a temporary addition that reverts after the twin bill.
  • Whitlock made no rehab appearances in the minors but is expected to slot immediately back in as Boston's top setup man ahead of closer Aroldis Chapman, giving Chad Tracy a proven bridge arm across 18-plus bullpen innings today.
  • Justin Slaten remains on the 15-day IL (right elbow inflammation) and was not part of Saturday's move.
ANTHONY, STORY: STILL WAITING
  • Roman Anthony and Trevor Story reportedly finished their rehab assignments this week, but neither was activated for today's doubleheader. Both are in Triple-A Worcester's own 4 PM Saturday lineup, and the Red Sox plan to evaluate next steps after that game.
  • Interim manager Chad Tracy declined to commit to a Sunday activation for either player: "It's just hard to say right now," he said Saturday, per Boston Globe beat writer Tim Healey.
  • Anthony has been out since May with a right hand/finger injury; Story is working back from sports hernia surgery. Both would add immediate lineup and defensive depth once activated.
Odds & Ends
  • Roster crunch easing: With Whitlock back and Anthony/Story rehab complete, Boston's injury picture is trending toward full health for the first time in months, right as rosters expand Sept. 1.
  • Catching depth: C Tomas Nido remains on a minor-league deal at Worcester (signed Thursday) as insurance behind Rutschman and Connor Wong; he is not on the 40-man roster.
  • Yankees' own headaches: Giancarlo Stanton (calf) is done for the year and Ryan Weathers (elbow flexor strain) is out for weeks; New York is otherwise getting healthier with Fried's activation.
  • Rosters expand Monday: Sept. 1 brings active rosters from 26 to 28 players, which should ease the roster math around Anthony's, Story's and Whitlock's returns.
