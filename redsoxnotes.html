A 23-year-old against a 28-year-old journeyman, and the age gap runs opposite to the résumés. Tolle leads the Red Sox in strikeouts with 134 — ten per nine innings — and his 1.083 WHIP is the best on the staff. His most recent notable outing was a 7-0 shutout win at Toronto on Aug. 13. Phillips, acquired from Philadelphia for cash, is the softer-contact opposite number: 7.1 strikeouts per nine against Tolle's 10.1, and a WHIP nearly three tenths higher. He has been serviceable rather than sharp, and the gap in baserunners allowed is the whole matchup. Tolle has not allowed more than three earned runs in a start since July. Miami's lineup is left-handed-vulnerable and Tolle is the second left-hander they see in two nights.
State of the Sox
  • Boston is 72-59 and has won four in a row, all of them after trailing. Third in the AL East, 6.0 games behind Tampa Bay (78-53), with the Yankees (74-56) in between.
  • The wild card is still the honest path, and the cushion grew again. Boston holds the second AL wild card, 6.5 games clear of the last spot (Cleveland, 66-66) and 2.5 behind the Yankees for the first. Houston is the first club out at 65-66.
  • Run differential is +101 (593 scored, 492 allowed) in 131 games. Baseball-Reference's Pythagorean record is 77-54 — five wins better than the real one, still the widest such gap in the AL East.
  • Thirty-one games left, 17 of them on the road. Eleven are against AL East opponents and every one is away from Fenway: four at Yankee Stadium, four at Camden Yards, three at Tampa Bay. Boston is finished with Toronto.
  • Two of the wild-card tiebreakers are Boston's own to take: three at Texas (Sept. 15-17) and three at home against Cleveland (Sept. 22-24).
THE EIGHTH-INNING TEAM:
  • Boston has scored 69 runs in the eighth inning this season — more than any other club in the major leagues. Monday's tying run came there, on a wild pitch, with Wilyer Abreu scoring from third after opening the inning with a double.
  • Three of the four wins in this streak followed the same script: trail late, tie it, take the lead in the final innings. Boston trailed 3-0 on Sunday, was tied 2-2 in the ninth on Saturday, and trailed 2-1 entering the eighth on Monday.
THE 1-FOR-14 WIN:
  • Here is a way to win a baseball game: go 1-for-14 with runners in scoring position, strand ten runners, commit the only error in the ballpark, and win anyway. Boston did all four Monday night.
  • The Marlins were even worse off — 2-for-18 with runners in scoring position and 14 men left on base. Twenty-four runners stranded between the two clubs in a game decided by two runs.
  • Boston's four runs came on a double, a wild pitch, a triple and a single — not one of them on a home run, and not one of them before the fifth inning. Both clubs went scoreless through four.
DOWN AT THE BOTTOM:
  • The 7-8-9 hitters — Duran, Gasper, Seigler — went 5-for-9 with two walks, three runs and both RBIs Monday. Interim manager Chad Tracy: "They set up innings for us all night."
  • Anthony Seigler's ninth-inning line drive down the right-field line was the first triple of his major-league career, off an 84.4-mph curveball, in his first start since Aug. 16. He is a second baseman — 42 of his 45 defensive appearances have come there and none behind the plate.
  • Mickey Gasper reached three times: a two-out RBI double in the fifth and walks in the seventh and ninth, the last of which became the winning run after Eli White pinch-ran for him.
THIRTY FOR THIRTY-EIGHT:
  • Aroldis Chapman worked a scoreless ninth for his 30th save. He is 38 years old, in his 17th major-league season, and carrying a 1.81 ERA with 11.7 strikeouts per nine across 46 appearances.
  • He is the oldest player on Boston's 40-man roster by two years. Tonight's starter, Payton Tolle, is 23 — born in November 2002, when Chapman was already 14. The two of them are the bookends of this roster, and both are left-handed.
THE CAVALRY REACHES WORCESTER:
  • Roman Anthony reports to Triple-A Worcester today after four games at Double-A Portland, where he went 3-for-11 with a double, a home run and two walks. MLB.com is streaming Worcester free tonight with Anthony and top prospect Arias both in the lineup.
  • Trevor Story (4-for-21 in six rehab games) and Isiah Kiner-Falefa (1-for-13) were examined at Fenway on Monday and are the two closest to activation. Tracy called this series "up in the air" for both.
  • Garrett Whitlock — 6-1, 2.05 ERA, 0.886 WHIP before the elbow — has resumed throwing bullpens and remains the single largest available upgrade to a bullpen that has already been the club's best department.
THE ROAD IS THE HOME:
  • Boston is 38-27 away from Fenway (.585) and 34-32 at home. That road mark is the second-best in the American League, behind only the Yankees' 41-28 — and Boston plays four at Yankee Stadium starting Friday.
  • Seventeen of the last 31 games are on the road, which for this particular club is not obviously bad news. Boston is also 17-8 against the AL Central and 24-19 in interleague play.
  • The counterweight sits in the other dugout: Miami is 41-26 at loanDepot park and 26-39 everywhere else — but 14-20 against left-handed starters. Tolle is the second lefty they have seen in two nights.
Odds & Ends

  • The 452: Statcast settled the argument on Contreras' Aug. 21 home run — 116 mph off the bat, 30-degree launch angle, 452 feet. It cleared everything in left field.

ROTATION & BULLPEN NOTES

  • Payton Tolle starts tonight. At 23 he leads the club with 134 strikeouts and owns the staff's best WHIP at 1.083. He has not allowed more than three earned runs in a start since July, and his last outing of note was a 7-0 shutout win at Toronto on Aug. 13.

  • Help may still arrive on this trip. Garrett Whitlock has resumed throwing bullpens and could be activated in Miami or New York; Justin Slaten is targeting September; Tanner Houck has been throwing live batting practice and may not be far from a rehab assignment.
  • Bello remains the bulk arm behind an opener. Alec Gamboa (1.40 ERA, 297 ERA+) is the opener of choice.
