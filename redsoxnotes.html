Suárez makes his 24th start looking to help Boston salvage a split of this four-game set and claim the season-series tiebreaker. Warren has posted an ERA north of 5.00 in three straight months and did not record a strikeout over 5 2/3 innings the last time these teams met, when Boston scored five runs on seven hits and three walks against him. Boston's deepest lineup in weeks — with Anthony and Story both back — gets the assignment of finishing the job.
State of the Sox
  • Boston is 74-62, third in the AL East, 7.5 games behind Tampa Bay (81-54) and 3.0 behind the Yankees (77-59) after Saturday's doubleheader split.
  • The Red Sox hold the second AL wild card at +6.0 over Cleveland (68-68, WC3) for the final postseason spot — a comfortable early-September cushion, though not yet a lock.
  • The Boston-New York season series is tied 6-6 with one game to play; today's winner owns the head-to-head tiebreaker, which could matter for playoff seeding.
  • Twenty-five games remain after today, including a return to Fenway Monday to open a three-game set against Seattle as rosters expand to 28 players.
  • A win today sends Boston home from the Bronx two games back of New York with a series split; a loss makes it four back heading into the season's final stretch.
SPLIT DECISION IN THE BRONX:
  • Boston answered back-to-back shutout losses with a statement 6-0 win in Saturday's Game 1, riding six scoreless innings from Jake Bennett. The offensive silence returned almost immediately: New York erased an early 1-0 Red Sox lead in Game 2 and pulled away with five runs in the eighth for a 9-2 win.
  • Brayan Bello, who piggybacked off Alec Gamboa in Game 2, allowed four runs over 4 1/3 innings — the most he's surrendered in any of his 13 relief appearances this season after a stretch of near-untouchable work (0.99 ERA over his first 12 outings out of the bullpen).
  • Raymond Burgos finished out a shaky eighth inning as Chad Tracy managed a taxed bullpen through 18-plus doubleheader innings; Burgos was optioned back to Worcester Sunday morning.
ANTHONY, STORY ARE BACK:
  • Roman Anthony is back in the lineup for the first time since May 4, when a partial tear of a tendon near his right ring finger sidelined him. He hit .280 with two home runs, two doubles and seven RBI over an eight-game rehab assignment split between Double-A Portland and Triple-A Worcester, and slots back in at leadoff and DH.
  • Trevor Story returns from sports hernia surgery that sidelined him since May 14; his rehab line was rougher (6-for-35, 11 K), so Chad Tracy is starting him lower in the order, eighth, rather than restoring him immediately as an everyday centerpiece.
  • Both players reportedly left Worcester's Polar Park with their major-league equipment bags Saturday night, the clearest signal their activations were imminent before Sunday's official announcement.
SOGARD'S SURGE:
  • Nick Sogard has three home runs since a week ago Sunday, when he'd never gone deep in the majors. His latest came off Max Fried in Saturday's Game 2 — a 351-foot opposite-field shot into the short right-field porch at Yankee Stadium that gave Boston its first lead.
  • Sogard also collected two hits and a strong relay throw to nab a runner at the plate in Saturday's Game 1 win, adding to a part-time role that continues to outperform its billing.
  • He starts at second base again today, batting ninth, as Andruw Monasterio moves to the bench with Trevor Story back at shortstop.
Odds & Ends
  • Roster crunch easing further: With Whitlock, Anthony and Story all activated inside a 48-hour window, Boston's injury picture is the healthiest it has been in months heading into the stretch run.
  • Catching depth: C Tomas Nido remains on a minor-league deal at Worcester as insurance behind Rutschman and Connor Wong; he is not on the 40-man roster.
  • Yankees' own headaches: Giancarlo Stanton (calf) is done for the year and Ryan Weathers (elbow flexor strain) is out for weeks; New York is otherwise getting healthier with Max Fried back in the rotation.
  • Rosters expand tomorrow: Sept. 1 brings active rosters from 26 to 28 players, which should ease any roster-math squeeze around today's Anthony/Story data flag (see Page 4).
