Two wins from two turned into two wins from two and a top-of-the-table place, City putting four goals past Crystal Palace at Selhurst Park with braces for Erling Haaland and Rayan Cherki either side of an own-goal deflection. Then came the transfer window's final 48 hours: Ayyoub Bouaddi's record £86m arrival was followed in the closing hours by Enzo Fernández for a joint British-record £125m and Iliman Ndiaye for £65m, while Omar Marmoush left on loan for Tottenham and Claudio Echeverri went out to Benfica. Newly promoted Coventry City, back in the top flight for the first time since 2001, arrive at the Etihad without a point or a goal from two matches and — per Opta — facing the toughest opening five fixtures in the division. This is the first City-Coventry meeting since the sides shared two second-tier games in 2001/02.

MATCH NOTES

State of the Club
  • City are top of the Premier League after matchweek 2 on 6 points, level with Arsenal, Hull City and Chelsea but ahead on goal difference and goals scored.
  • All four of the pace-setters have a maximum six points; the table is separated almost entirely on goal difference this early.
  • The Maresca era is now 2-0-1 across all competitions: the Community Shield defeat to Arsenal, then wins over Bournemouth and Crystal Palace.
  • Saturday is City's third home Premier League fixture in four attempts this calendar year's start and the last domestic match before Tuesday's Champions League opener at Porto.
Official Premier League table via mancity.com/tables, last updated 1 September 2026.
Matchweek 2 Recap — Crystal Palace 1-4 City
  • Friday 28 August, Selhurst Park. Erling Haaland looped in a headed opener from an Antoine Semenyo cross on 17 minutes after City had already hit the woodwork and seen two other half-chances go begging.
  • Rayan Cherki doubled the lead early in the second half (54') with what the club called "the most impudent of finishes," then restored a two-goal cushion (59') within three minutes of Palace pulling one back.
  • Palace's goal, on 56 minutes, came from a Yeremy Pino 25-yard free-kick that cannoned in off Gianluigi Donnarumma — recorded as a Donnarumma own goal rather than a Pino strike.
  • Haaland completed his brace late on, finishing at the back post after City had already introduced three debutants/near-debutants — Ayyoub Bouaddi, Ryan McAidoo and Kaden Braithwaite — from the bench.
  • Maresca: "We were in control. We scored four and could probably have scored more and we didn't concede chances … We were good on the ball but also very, very good off the ball."
Source: mancity.com match report, Paul Brown, 28 Aug 2026, 21:55 BST. Quotes reproduced as published.
Deadline Day Bonanza — Storyline of the Week
  • The summer window shut at 22:00 GMT on Tuesday 1 September (Premier League clubs). City's business ran to the wire on both sides of the ledger.
  • In: Ayyoub Bouaddi (Lille, 26 Aug, £81.3m fixed + £4.3m add-ons — a record fee for a teenager in Premier League history), Iliman Ndiaye (Everton, 1 Sep, £65m), Enzo Fernández (Chelsea, 1 Sep, £125m — a fee that equals the British transfer record).
  • Out: Omar Marmoush (Tottenham, season-long loan with a reported £60m obligation to buy, 27 Aug), Claudio Echeverri (Benfica, loan, confirmed around deadline day).
  • Fernández reunites with Enzo Maresca, his manager at Chelsea, and takes the No.17 shirt previously worn at City by Kevin De Bruyne — his favoured Nos. 24, 8 and 5 were already taken.
  • Fernández: "Manchester City are the most successful club in England in recent times … I could not be happier to be here, and I am ready for the challenge of helping City continue winning trophies."
Sources: mancity.com signing articles for Bouaddi, Ndiaye and Fernández (26 Aug & 1 Sep 2026); Sky Sports and Al Jazeera on the Marmoush loan (27–28 Aug 2026); the Echeverri loan is sourced to a mancity.com headline link only — full article not retrieved this run, flagged on page 5.
Coventry: The Newly Promoted Visitors
  • Coventry return to the top flight for the first time since 2001, having won the Championship by 11 points with 97 goals scored.
  • They have played two, lost two, scored none — 3-0 at home to Arsenal, then 0-1 at home to Hull City — and are one of only three sides yet to score this season.
  • Opta rates their run of opening fixtures as the toughest in the division and gives them a 27.9% chance of an immediate relegation.
  • Manager Frank Lampard had a brief playing spell at City in 2013/14 under Manuel Pellegrini, scoring eight times in 38 appearances before retiring from the Premier League with a goal in his final game.
  • This is the first competitive meeting between the clubs since 2001/02 (Division One); City won 4-2 at Maine Road, Coventry won the return 4-3. Of 10 previous top-flight meetings, City have won two and Coventry four, mostly in the 1990s.
Sources: mancity.com "Analysing Coventry ahead of Saturday's Premier League clash" (Jack Mumford, 1 Sep 2026); mancity.com/tables (1 Sep 2026) for Coventry's record.

SECTION NOTES

The Midfield, Finally Addressed
  • City opened the season with Guéhi, a centre-half, filling the Rodri-shaped hole in midfield alongside Anderson — a stopgap Maresca used in both August matches.
  • The window's final 48 hours brought two long-term answers: Ayyoub Bouaddi, an 18-year-old defensive midfielder signed as the direct Rodri succession plan, and Enzo Fernández, a ready-made Premier League-proven No.8/6 reunited with his former Chelsea manager.
  • Mateo Kovačić, who reached 100 City appearances at Selhurst Park, remains the incumbent specialist option whenever Maresca wants a deep-lying holder rather than a goal-side contributor.
  • The selection question this week is no longer whether City have a recognised midfielder available — it's which two (or three) of Anderson, Fernández, Bouaddi, Guéhi and Kovačić start.
Sources: mancity.com teamsheets, 23 & 28 Aug 2026; mancity.com signing articles for Bouaddi and Fernández; Evening Standard/Yahoo Sports Coventry preview, 2 Sep 2026, for the "question marks" framing, presented here as the outlet's own characterisation.
