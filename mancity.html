City won an ill-tempered Manchester derby 1-0 at Old Trafford on Sunday, but the story afterward became as much about officiating as the football. Phil Foden was sent off in the 23rd minute for kicking out at Bruno Fernandes after the United captain had twice caught him while he was down — Michael Oliver's red card stood on VAR review, and Enzo Maresca said afterward he thought Fernandes's own initial contact merited a card too. City held out with ten men until the hour mark, when Erling Haaland turned in Joško Gvardiol's deflected cross at the back post after a lengthy VAR check for a Enzo Fernández offside in the build-up. The goal stood on the night; two days later, referees' body Pro Ref publicly acknowledged an "error of judgement" and apologised to Manchester United, saying the incident should have been sent to Michael Oliver for an on-field review. United, who hit the woodwork twice through Marcus Rashford and Kobbie Mainoo, remain winless in four matches under mounting pressure on Michael Carrick. Attention now turns to City's Carabao Cup defence at home to Norwich on Thursday, before Sunderland visit the Etihad on Sunday.

Manchester Derby — Man Utd 0-1 Man City
  • Sunday 13 September, Old Trafford. Phil Foden was sent off in the 23rd minute for kicking out at Bruno Fernandes after a coming-together in which Fernandes caught Foden twice on the ground; Michael Oliver's red stood on VAR review.
  • United hit the woodwork either side of half-time — Marcus Rashford before the break, Kobbie Mainoo after it — but couldn't find a way past a resolute, ten-man City rearguard.
  • Erling Haaland turned home Joško Gvardiol's deflected low cross on 60 minutes; the goal stood after a lengthy VAR check despite Enzo Fernández's offside position in the build-up.
  • Match stats: United led possession 55-45% and shots 16-6, but only 3 of those were on target to City's 2; City's underlying xG (1.1) edged United's (1.0) despite the shot count.
  • Cards: Foden (red, 23'), Maresca (yellow, technical area, 45+2'), Gvardiol (yellow, 79'), Enzo Fernández (yellow, 80'), Harry Maguire (yellow, 81').
  • Maresca post-match: "Phil's card is a red card, but I also think the same action is a red card by Bruno [Fernandes]." On the tactics with ten men: "If we just wait for them and don't counter-attack, the game would have become more difficult for us." He later called the focus on Haaland's goal over City's ten-man performance "quite poor."
Sources: mancity.com match report and post-match press conference coverage; Sports Mole match report, highlights and stats (13 Sep 2026); ESPN team stats and Maresca reaction piece; OptaJoe via Sports Mole for the historical scoring/red-card stats.
Storyline of the Week — Pro Ref's Apology
  • Two days after the derby, referees' body Pro Ref (formerly PGMOL) publicly acknowledged an "error of judgement" over Haaland's winning goal and said it had apologised to Manchester United.
  • VAR Matthew Donohue and assistant VAR Blake Antrobus judged Enzo Fernández, who was offside, not to be interfering with play as he surged toward Gvardiol's cross without touching the ball; Pro Ref said the incident should instead have been sent to Michael Oliver for an on-field review.
  • The admission does not change the result — the 1-0 scoreline stands — but has fuelled fresh scrutiny of Michael Carrick's position after a fourth match without a win to open United's league season.
